Thoughts on forgiveness

Fri, Oct 9, 2026 · 02:00 · Pittsburgh

Date: Fri, 9 Oct 2026 01:49:07 -0400
Subject: Thoughts on forgiveness
From: Rosalyn Tosh <rtosh@andrew.cmu.edu>
To: Gina Casalegno <ginac@andrew.cmu.edu>

Hi Gina.

I was sitting for about 20 minutes tonight waiting for the train. I was in long sleeves and it wasn't that cold. But I'm sick right now (from an ordinary cold), and I was so tired I could barely think under the brain fog, and I'm so thin now that all the cold made it to my bones before the train finally came up to the platform.

I sat in the train feeling small in a way I never have in my life. Physically small, because I have lost so much weight, but also small in the universe. I felt an overwhelming wish that some force beyond my comprehension would lift me up as a cold, tired, sick little emaciated creature and cradle me in its arms and nurse me back to health, and set me back down, safe and comfortable and whole again. I cannot share this with anyone because I am the one who is strong and optimistic and powerful.

If I do make the mistake of updating my support network on how I'm actually doing, they offer physical resources or sympathy, both of which I'm not interested in taking from them. Those should go to people who feel just as desperate as I do, but who will not at some point in a few months be just as strong and alive as they once were, as will be the case for me, because I have the resilience that comes from the most basic privilege. It makes me think of the kids at CMU (and we are all kids. I am a kid, my classmates are kids, my TAs are kids, we're all barely starting the parts of our lives that are our own) who have felt the same physical stress and weakness that I do right now, but without the purpose that I have.

I forgive you for what you are doing to me. You know that you are torturing me — that you are depriving me of food, that you are putting me under immense physical and mental stress, that you denied me a place to live, that you are denying me places to study for the classes I am trying to take. But I think you know I'll survive it. What I do not forgive you for is the people I think about who did not make it out of CMU. You have drowned students under crushing burdens, without securing the resources and support they needed to make it through that. During midterms and finals, it is hard to find food on campus, even as a student with a dining plan and full access to campus. When students are overwhelmed by stress, they cannot see CaPS without admitting to (or faking) immediate risk to themselves. When students are underwater in deadlines and pressure (pressure not rooted in any actual danger, just words on paper), they have crushingly limited options to reduce the pressure they're under (and if you're out of vouchers, you might have no options). I have watched every person I love at CMU be broken down to what will probably be the lowest lows they will ever experience.

As the person who runs Student Affairs, you are ultimately responsible for the university's disregard for its students. You can remember the name of every student you've ever met, but you somehow haven't managed to protect their lives in the slightest. You've worked for CMU for what, two or three decades? And CaPS is still undersized by a factor of 10, students still lack reliable access to nutrition, programs for marginalized or poor students are all disasters, and the only option for overcommitted students continues to be giving up in one form or another. You could claim you don't have the power or funding to fix any of those, but you work 20 feet from the people who do.

I didn't plan on saying "free Palestine" being the issue that would cross our paths once I returned from my suspension. My plan — to make use of my voice while contributing to the campus I love — was to make students' wellbeing my issue, and to dig up all the data, and to write op-eds, and to do the work of focusing attention onto the things that I see crushing the people who walk out of Carnegie Cup joyfully anticipating this chapter of their lives. When the Fence stuff started up, and I was suffering as intensely as I was under the restrictions you put in place and the actions you took, I assumed it all had to be a mistake. I described it all to you, we resolved multiple massive miscommunications about my situation, and then...nothing. You continued to subject me to what you had every reason to believe could kill me. You're supposed to urgently visit a doctor if you lose 5% of your body weight unintentionally. You canceled my doctor's appointment when I hit 15%. I no longer believe students' wellbeing is an issue we could've found common ground on. You've shown me through your actions that you will bury a student if it serves the interests of your employer.

Usually when someone of my political persuasion says that someone has "blood on their hands," there is a lot of indirection. They make the weapons, or they make abstract political decisions, or they don't pull the lever in some trolley problem somewhere. But you, Gina, have had 20 years to correct the fact that CMU's rates of depression and suicide are far outside the acceptable limits, and to cope you have built so much distance between yourself and the destruction you wreak on my classmates. They came to this campus as giddy 19 year-olds and some will leave in ambulances because of the direct consequences of your decisions and your inaction.

You are a murderer.

--

Rosalyn Tosh
